## The design decision that mattered

The upgrade itself was a facilities project. The security outcome came from one architectural
choice made early: the camera and access-control endpoints would not share a broadcast domain with
the people who read email.

IP cameras and door controllers are long-lived, rarely patched, and frequently shipped with
credentials that nobody rotates. Treating them as untrusted from the start is cheap. Retrofitting
that assumption after they are installed and working is not — it means touching a system the
facilities team now depends on.

## Approach

**Sequence the vendors against a hard gate.** PoE switch procurement and availability were managed
directly against the installation schedule, so that network capacity was on site before the
integrator needed it. This is the unglamorous half of infrastructure work and the half that
determines whether a project lands on time.

**Segment by default.** VLANs were designed and configured to isolate security and camera traffic
from production and user networks, so that a compromised or simply misbehaving camera endpoint had
no path to the systems that carry real data.

**Stage against live operations.** Changes were staged and validated while the facility continued
to run, rather than depending on an outage window that a working facility could not give.

## Result

The upgrade completed with zero operational downtime, improved physical security coverage, and an
entire category of IoT-class device permanently contained behind a segmentation boundary.
