## Why this one matters most

The single-environment work on this site demonstrates depth. This one demonstrates the thing that
is harder to teach: carrying many stakeholders concurrently and making a defensible call about
what each of them should spend, change, and defer.

## Approach

**Standardise identity first.** Fragmented credentials were the common failure across the book —
every client had accumulated a different set of local accounts, shared logins, and half-migrated
directories. Integrating Okta with Microsoft 365 to deliver single sign-on and MFA collapsed that
sprawl into one controllable access layer, which is the highest-leverage security control
available in a small organisation.

**Treat the perimeter as an estate, not a device.** SonicWall and Meraki firewall deployments and
VMware virtualization environments were managed as a portfolio, so that a lesson learned at one
client became the default at the next.

**Sequence migrations per client, not per calendar.** On-premises to Azure and Office 365 moves
were scoped independently. A regulated client with a data-residency constraint and a twenty-person
firm with a hardware refresh due are not the same project, and pretending otherwise is how MSP
engagements go badly.

**Make the work visible.** Delivery ran through ConnectWise so that effort, state, and history
were attributable per client — which matters for billing, but matters more for being able to
answer "what changed?" six months later.

## Result

A multi-vertical client book operated concurrently, with modernised identity, perimeter, and cloud
footprints delivered client by client rather than as a single forced march.
